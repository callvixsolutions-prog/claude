Free Pest Control Answering Service Vendor Scorecard

Compare answering-service providers consistently. This printable scorecard puts the same 25 questions to every vendor and scores them the same way across call quality, booking rules, escalation, reporting and pricing.

Download the scorecard (PDF, 5 pages, 273 KB) PDF · 5 pages · 273 KB · free, no sign-up
By Callvix SolutionsPublished September 2026

Disclosure: this scorecard was created by Callvix Solutions, a human-answered call answering service for U.S. pest-control companies. It is vendor-neutral and can be used to evaluate Callvix or any other provider.

How to use this scorecard

1

Print one copy per provider

Fill in the vendor name, reviewer, date and the plan or price they proposed.

2

Ask all 25 questions

Ask each vendor for a direct answer, an example and, where possible, a live demonstration. Tick Clear, Partial or Unclear and note the evidence.

3

Score the five categories

Using your notes, score each category from 0 to 2 for a total out of 10.

4

Compare, then pilot

Compare totals and decision notes side by side, then test your strongest candidate in a controlled pilot before forwarding all calls.

Preview the scorecard

This is what the PDF contains, so you can use it without downloading. Each question is explained in detail in our guide to the 25 questions to ask before hiring an answering service.

Part A: record an answer to each question

Answer key. Clear: a direct answer backed by an example, document or demonstration. Partial: answered, but vague or not yet verified. Unclear: no answer, or the provider could not explain it.

Part 1 Caller experience and brand representation

  1. Will calls be answered in our company name?
  2. Who actually answers the call?
  3. How are agents trained on pest control conversations?
  4. Can the handler adapt when the caller goes off script?

Part 2 Qualification and intake accuracy

  1. What information is collected on every new inquiry?
  2. How is the service area checked?
  3. How does the service distinguish a new lead from an existing customer?
  4. How are unclear pest descriptions handled?

Part 3 Booking rules and schedule control

  1. Can the service book directly into our calendar or CRM?
  2. Which services may be booked without office approval?
  3. How are technician skills, territories and drive time respected?
  4. What can the handler say about price?
  5. How are cancellations, reschedules and recurring-service calls handled?

Part 4 Urgency, safety and escalation

  1. What counts as urgent—and who decides?
  2. What are agents prohibited from promising?
  3. How is the on-call person reached?
  4. What happens after hours, on weekends and during holidays?

Part 5 Technology, access and handoffs

  1. Which systems can the provider work in?
  2. What access will the service require?
  3. What does the office or technician receive after each call?
  4. How are system outages handled?

Part 6 Quality control and accountability

  1. Can we review call recordings, summaries and outcomes?
  2. How is quality measured and corrected?
  3. Which reports will we receive?

Part 7 Pricing and contract terms

  1. What will a realistic busy-season month cost?

Part B: score the five categories

Vendor scorecard: score each provider from 0 to 2 in each category, for a total out of 10
Category0 points1 point2 points
Pest-control readinessGeneric or unclearSome relevant experienceDocumented pest-specific workflow
Booking controlTakes messages onlyLimited bookingBooks by your written rules
EscalationAd hocBasic escalationTested, multi-step escalation plan
VisibilityMinimal reportingSummaries or recordingsCalls, outcomes and QA are reviewable
Pricing clarityVague or incompleteMost charges explainedComplete busy-season cost example
  • 9–10: Strong candidate for a controlled pilot.
  • 7–8: Promising, but resolve the weak category in writing.
  • 5–6: High implementation risk; test carefully before forwarding all calls.
  • 0–4: The provider is not ready to operate as your front office.

The total is a comparison aid, not a guarantee of service quality. It is a screening tool, not a substitute for a pilot: a provider should still prove it can follow your actual rules on real or simulated calls.

Part C: summarize the decision

The last page has space for each provider’s strengths, risks, unanswered questions and next step, plus a table to compare up to three providers by total score and weakest category.
