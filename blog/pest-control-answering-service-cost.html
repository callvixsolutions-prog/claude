Pricing guide

How much does a pest control answering service cost in 2026?

By Callvix Solutions8 min readPublished 1 July 2026Updated 20 October 2026

If you run a pest control company, an unanswered call can mean a missed booking. An answering service can help — but pricing models differ, and the wrong one can make a busy month cost more than you planned. Here’s a straight breakdown of what a pest control answering service costs in 2026, the two ways usage is billed, and how to compare them without surprises on the invoice.

The short answer: Answering services commonly bill usage per minute or per call, on top of a monthly base that may include an allowance and separate feature fees. PATLive’s published pricing is per minute; two points on its published ladder are $49/month with no included minutes ($2.99 per minute) and $349/month for 200 included minutes ($1.99 per additional minute). Smith.ai’s published pricing is per call, from $300/month for 30 included calls ($11.50 per additional call). Which suits you depends on your own call length and volume, so compare total cost at the volume you actually expect — not the headline rate.

The two billing units, explained

Two common usage units are minutes and calls. Plans may combine a monthly base with an included allowance, usage charges and separate feature fees. Understanding which one you are being quoted, and what the monthly base already includes, is the single most important thing you can do before signing up.

1. Per-minute billing

You pay for every minute an agent spends on your calls, usually on top of a monthly base that includes a set allowance of minutes. As a published example, PATLive’s published pricing lists a long ladder of plans. Two points on it: $49/month with no included minutes, charging $2.99 per minute, and $349/month with 200 minutes included and $1.99 per additional minute. The ladder continues well beyond that — the more minutes you commit to, the lower the per-minute rate. Rates differ by provider, so check the plan you are actually being quoted.

The catch for pest control: some calls take time. A homeowner with a rodent problem may describe the situation, ask whether treatment is safe for kids and pets, check pricing and book a time. Check your own call records for average length, and ask how the provider rounds minutes — in a busy season, a per-minute bill grows with both the number and the length of calls.

2. Per-call billing

Per-call plans charge by billable call rather than by the minute. Check the provider’s definition of a billable call, any duration limits, and separate charges for transfers, booking or follow-up. Within those rules, a long call costs the same as a short one, which makes the bill easier to forecast. What differs between providers on this model is the monthly base, how many calls that base includes, and the rate charged once the allowance is used.

As a published example, Smith.ai’s published pricing lists three plans, each pairing a base with its own allowance and overage rate: $300/month for 30 calls with $11.50 per additional call, $810/month for 90 calls at $10.50, and $2,100/month for 300 calls at $8.50. The overage rate only applies once the included calls are used, so it is not the price of every call — which is exactly why comparing two providers on their headline per-call figures alone will mislead you.

To compare any two per-call providers properly, take the call volume you actually expect, work out each one’s total at that volume including base and overage, and then check what coverage hours and features each total buys.

The hidden costs to ask about

The sticker price is rarely the whole story. Before you sign, ask every provider these questions:

  • Is there a setup fee? Some providers charge one and some do not, and it is not always on the pricing page. Ask before you sign.
  • Are spam and wrong-number calls billed? Ask whatever the billing unit: on per-minute plans they use allowance, and on per-call plans they may count as billable calls.
  • What does a realistic busy-season month cost? Ask for an estimate at 2–3× your normal call volume, not just the base. Our guide to pest call volume by season and region shows when those months tend to fall.
  • Is there a long-term contract? Month-to-month protects you if the service underdelivers.
  • What outcome are you buying? A message, a pending appointment request and a confirmed booking are different purchases. Compare the outcome, its full cost, the follow-up work it leaves for your team and who owns the next step.

Price is only one part of the decision. For the questions on booking rules, escalation, reporting and access, work through our 25-question vendor checklist and record each provider’s answers on the answering service scorecard.

How to think about cost the right way

Start with the total monthly cost at the call volume you actually expect — the base, billable calls or minutes, and any add-ons — and compare providers at the same volume and coverage. Then weigh that cost against the extra jobs you would book: the margin on completed, paid work, not just the number of bookings, since some callers would have reached you another way. Our missed-calls guide walks through that estimate with your own numbers.

That is why the billing unit matters as much as the headline number: it decides whether a longer call, or a busier month, changes what you pay.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a pest control answering service cost?

It depends on the billing unit and the volume included. Published examples verified in October 2026: PATLive bills per minute, with $49/month including no minutes ($2.99 per minute) and $349/month including 200 minutes ($1.99 per additional minute) — two points on a longer ladder of plans; Smith.ai bills per call, from $300/month for 30 included calls ($11.50 per additional call). Callvix charges $399/month plus $2.99 per answered inbound call with no allowance, so every inbound call is charged at the same rate.

Is per-call or per-minute billing better?

It depends on your call length and volume. Per-minute billing rises with call duration as well as call count; per-call billing rises with the number of billable calls, within any duration limits the provider sets, which makes the bill easier to forecast. Compare the total monthly cost at your own expected volume, including allowances, rounding, required features and the coverage hours you need. Then compare that full cost with the contribution margin on the additional completed, paid work it could support, which is your own number rather than the provider’s.

What hidden fees should I watch for?

Setup fees, per-minute overages above your allowance, spam calls billed against your minutes, and long-term contracts with early-termination penalties. Always ask what a realistic busy-season month actually costs.
