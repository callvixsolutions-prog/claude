What missed calls really cost a pest control business

By Callvix Solutions6 min readPublished 10 July 2026Updated 6 October 2026

Every pest control owner knows the feeling: you get out of a crawl space, check your phone, and see two missed calls. You call back — no answer. They already booked someone else. Missed calls aren't a minor annoyance; for a pest control business, they're one of the biggest silent leaks in your revenue. Here's how to put a real number on it.

The short version: we are not going to quote you an industry statistic about voicemail, because the numbers that circulate are mostly vendor-published and impossible to verify. What we can do is give you the arithmetic. With four numbers pulled from your own phone system and CRM, you can estimate what unanswered calls are costing you — using your data, not someone else’s averages.

Why pest control loses more than most trades

Two things make missed calls especially expensive in pest control:

  • Urgency. Wasps by the door, rodents in the attic, ants across the kitchen — people want it handled today. If you don't answer, the next company will.
  • Recurring value. A one-time call often becomes a quarterly plan. Losing the first call means losing the whole relationship, not just one job.

The general principle is documented outside pest control: in “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads” (Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, Harvard Business Review, March 2011), the authors report that most companies respond to inbound enquiries far more slowly than those leads remain realistically contactable. We have not reproduced that study’s specific figures here, because they come from online B2B lead forms rather than inbound pest-control phone calls. The direction of travel is the useful part — on an urgent inbound call, the practical window is often just the time it takes the caller to dial the next result on Google.

The simple missed-call math

You can estimate your own lost revenue with three numbers:

  • A = missed calls per month (check your phone or call logs)
  • B = your booking rate on answered calls — take it from your own CRM, not from an industry average
  • C = your average value of a first job — again, your own figure, not an industry average

Your rough monthly lost revenue is simply A × B × C.

A worked example

InputValue
Missed calls per month (A)40
Booking rate (B) — illustrative40%
Average first job (C) — illustrative$500
Lost first-job revenue / month≈ $8,000
Lost revenue / year≈ $96,000

And that only counts the first job. Some share of those callers would have become recurring accounts, so the true cost is higher — but by how much depends entirely on your own average account value and retention, which is why we have not put an industry figure on it.

Where the calls are actually being missed

For most pest control companies, missed calls cluster in three predictable gaps:

  • During service hours, when you and your techs are on-site and can't get to the phone.
  • After hours and weekends, when the office is closed but pest problems aren't — where a written after-hours coverage and escalation plan decides what happens to each call.
  • During busy-season overflow, when calls come in faster than one person can answer.

How to plug the leak

The fix isn't "try to answer faster" — you're already busy running jobs. It's making sure a capable person is always there to pick up, qualify the issue, and book the appointment on your behalf. That's exactly what a pest control answering service does: your phone is covered in your company name whether you're in a crawl space, driving, or closed for the night.

Frequently asked questions

How many pest control leads are lost to missed calls?

There is no reliable published figure specific to pest control, and the numbers that circulate widely are mostly vendor-published. The practical answer is to measure it yourself: count unanswered inbound calls in your phone system over a month and compare it against how many of those numbers ever became customers.

How do I calculate the cost of missed calls?

Multiply your monthly missed calls by your booking rate, then by the average value of a job. As an illustrative example only, 40 missed calls × a 40% booking rate × a $500 average first job would be about $8,000 in lost first-job revenue per month. Use your own booking rate and job value rather than these placeholder figures.

Why do so many callers not leave a voicemail?

Pest problems feel urgent. Homeowners want help now, not a callback later, so if no one answers they immediately try the next company.
